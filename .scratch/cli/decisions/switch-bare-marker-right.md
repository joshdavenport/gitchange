Core's `switch` is one locked state write — no internal refresh, no `OpOutcome` — and that is the decided shape, not a gap. ADR 0005 already lists the ops whose internal refreshes persist as assign, stage, commit; the TUI's instant claim of the unassigned pool after a switch belongs to the _engine's_ post-op refresh, not the op, so op parity holds. A post-marker refresh inside `switch` was rejected: `switch <name>` would sweep every pre-existing unassigned hunk — other actors' fresh work included — the moment the marker moves. A pre-marker refresh was rejected harder: the agent skill's opening `switch unassigned` under another actor's active changelist would claim the pending pool into _that_ changelist. Deferred claim is the reads-never-persist decision's accepted cost, and the TUI's claim-at-switch behaviour composes: `switch <name>` then `refresh`. `switch`'s receipt is the echo alone, composed in core (see the receipt-carrier decision).
