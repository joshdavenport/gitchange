`<path>[:<hunk-id>]` in path positions and `--containing <line>` are gitchange's single addressing language: every hunk-addressing verb — `assign`, `add`/`stage`, `unstage` — speaks it with `assign`'s rules verbatim, so a future verb inherits the grammar and decides only its name and refusal semantics. The split: addressing is the language, refusals are the verb's. `--containing` therefore resolves over every hunk in the path's universe first and checks the verb's scope on the unique match — scope-filtered matching would make the same value quietly mean different hunks under different verbs. For `add`, the changelist positional is a consistency guard: an addressed hunk owned elsewhere refuses, naming the actual owner (a one-step exact retry), and unassigned hunks stage via `add unassigned`. This is deliberately stricter than the TUI, whose per-hunk `space` ignores ownership (#97's scoping stopped at file rows): the TUI's selection is one human's statement of intent; the CLI's caller is an agent under ADR 0015's explicit-mode contract, and a silent cross-ownership stage is what `add`'s invariant rules out.
